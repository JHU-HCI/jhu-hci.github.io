## Abstract

Robots can serve as physical interfaces with the world, especially for users with limited mobility. However, people have a wide variety of preferences for how these robots should behave. My research focuses on how we can adapt a robot's appearance, physical interactions, and social interactions to individual users? needs and preferences. First I will present my work on how we can use design metaphors to understand general user expectations for how robots should act. Next, I will describe the Bimanual Arm Reaching Test with a Robot (BARTR), which we developed to learn personalized movement models of post-stroke participants. Finally, I will present my work on using concepts from exploratory search to facilitate end-user design of robot signals. By adapting these three aspects of robots that interact with people, we can develop robots that holistically assist users.

## Speaker Bio

Nathan Dennler is a fifth-year PhD student at the University of Southern California, advised by Maja Matarić and Stefanos Nikolaidis. Their research focuses on developing assistive robots that adapt to user preferences. Nathan is an active organizer in the Queer in Robotics and Queer in AI organizations, and the recipient of an NSF GRFP. Previously, Nathan received Bachelor's Degrees in Computer Science and Robotics Engineering from Worcester Polytechnic Institute, where they worked with the ASSISTments lab on Intelligent Tutoring Systems.
