## Abstract

As social media platforms have grown in popularity, they have evolved into complex advertising and influencer ecosystems, creating new trust and safety challenges. This talk synthesizes three studies examining targeted advertising, scam discourse, and the governance of generative AI (GenAI) across global and youth contexts. First, drawing on interviews and surveys across South Asia and the US, we show how sociocultural and contextual norms shape privacy perceptions and perceived benefits of targeted ads, challenging country-level assumptions about culture. Second, through qualitative analysis of creator-reported scam experiences on TikTok, we identify platform-specific scam typologies and examine how creators and communities collaboratively construct anti-scam knowledge. Third, using the Governing Knowledge Commons?Contextual Integrity (GKC-CI) framework, we analyze policies from platforms, GenAI model providers, and ad services, revealing governance gaps—particularly in protecting young adults from GenAI-enabled manipulative advertising. Together, these studies highlight the socio-technical dimensions of digital trust and propose design, community, and regulatory interventions to create safer social media ecosystems.

## Speaker Bio

Smirity Kaushik is a postdoctoral associate at The George Washington University, Dept. of Computer Science, in the Usable Security and Privacy Lab with Dr. Adam Aviv. She completed her Ph.D. in Information Sciences at the University of Illinois Urbana Champaign co-supervised by Dr. Yang Wang and Dr. Madelyn Sanfilippo. Her research intersects human-centered security, privacy, AI safety, and governance. Through her research, she aims to make people's experiences with emerging technologies safe, trustworthy, and empowering. Her current work focuses on AI safety in social media, human perception of Trustworthy AI, and accessible computing.
