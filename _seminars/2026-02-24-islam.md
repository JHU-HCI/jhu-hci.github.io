## Abstract

Many chronic and neurological health conditions require frequent monitoring, yet traditional clinic-based assessment remains costly, episodic, and difficult to access for many populations. At the same time, everyday devices such as smartphones, microphones, and wearables can capture rich behavioral and physiological signals in natural settings. The central challenge is not data availability, but how to transform noisy, real-world signals into reliable, interpretable, and useful health information for decision-making. In this talk, I will present my research agenda on human-centered multimodal AI for remote health assessment. My work integrates video, audio, and wearable data with uncertainty-aware modeling to account for variability in data quality, user behavior, and sensing environments. Parkinson's disease serves as a motivating case study throughout the talk, as its symptoms naturally manifest across multiple modalities and highlight the need for robust fusion and careful evaluation. I will discuss a set of algorithmic and system-level design principles that enable trustworthy multimodal health sensing outside the clinic. I will also cover large-scale validation, clinician-aligned evaluation, and user-centered studies that assess usability and acceptance across diverse populations. Finally, I will outline future directions in lightweight remote monitoring and privacy-conscious personalized health agents driven by large language models.

## Speaker Bio

Md Saiful Islam is a Computer Science Ph.D. candidate at the University of Rochester, specializing in human-centered AI and digital health. His research develops multimodal AI systems that transform everyday signals, such as video, audio, and wearable data, into reliable digital biomarkers for remote health assessment, with a focus on movement disorders like Parkinson's disease. By combining technical innovation with close clinical collaboration, he aims to make healthcare more proactive, personalized, and accessible.
Before starting his Ph.D., he was an Assistant Professor at Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET). His work has been published in venues such as npj Digital Medicine, New England Journal of Medicine AI, AAAI, CHI, ACL, and EMNLP, and has been supported by the Google Ph.D. Fellowship in Health Research. He has also collaborated with industry partners, including Samsung Research America and Google DeepMind, to translate sensing and modeling techniques into real-world health applications.
