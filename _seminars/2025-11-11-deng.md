## Abstract

There has been growing interest among both practitioners and researchers in engaging end users in AI auditing and red-teaming to draw upon their unique knowledge and lived experiences. However, we still know little about how to effectively scaffold end users in conducting audits that produce actionable insights for AI practitioners. At the same time, automated red-teaming has the potential to complement human efforts by scaling evaluations and reducing the need for humans to be exposed to harmful content. Yet, current automated approaches have not adequately considered the role of identity.

In this talk, I present two systems?WeAudit and PersonaTeaming?that explore new ways to coordinate and enhance AI auditing and red-teaming efforts. Through these two case studies, I aim to demonstrate empirically grounded approaches to addressing on-the-ground needs for responsible AI in industry, and to highlight future directions for more effective and sustainable responsible AI and AI safety practices.

## Speaker Bio

Wesley Hanwen Deng's research focuses on responsible AI (RAI), AI safety, and human-AI collaboration. His current work aims at building tools and processes to support the design, development, and governance of safer, more responsible AI systems, with a specific focus on AI auditing, red-teaming, and impact assessment. Wesley's work has been published at top-tier conferences in human-computer interaction and responsible AI, such as CHI, CSCW, FAccT, NeurIPS, HCOMP, and AIES, winning three best paper awards (top 1% of submissions) and one honorable mention (top 3% of submissions). He has also organized a series of workshops at these conferences to foster interdisciplinary collaboration around RAI, AI auditing, and AI red-teaming. His work has been recognized through awards such as Microsoft AI & Society Fellowship and K&L Gates Presidential Fellowship. During his time at CMU, Wesley has led or co-led numerous grant proposals, securing over $750,000 in funding. Prior to CMU, Wesley earned his undergraduate degree with the highest honor in Computer Science from UC Berkeley, where he also received an EECS research honor.
