## Abstract

Integrating social robots into everyday family life requires attention to more than technical capability — it calls for a deep understanding of family practices, relationships, and changing expectations over time. In this talk, I present a family-centered approach to designing interactions for social robots, drawing on participatory design, long-term field studies, and bridging theory between HRI and human ecology. Through three interrelated studies, (1) on family expectations, (2) patterns of in-home use of robots, and (3) a framework for family-robot interaction, I illustrate how families shape and are shaped by their interactions with social robots. This work advances both design strategies and theoretical insights for creating technologies that are not only individually adaptive but socially and ecologically embedded.
