## Abstract

AI systems can now write code, synthesize research, reason through multi-step problems, and operate autonomously. But by optimizing for task completion, AI often fails to empower humans to think, plan, learn, and create effectively: users struggle to verify AI outputs, delegate tasks without knowing what AI does well, and gradually lose their own skills as they rely on AI-generated content.
In this talk, I propose a shift toward cognitive alignment: building AI systems that model and adapt to how humans think, augmenting rather than burdening human capability. I demonstrate this through three directions: (1) adaptive systems that model users' cognitive context to provide proactive support, such as anticipating researchers' information needs or scaffolding children's understanding through dialogue, (2) interpretable evaluation methods that assess model capabilities by decomposing tasks into human cognitive sub-steps, and (3) simulation methods that model diverse cognitive strategies, enabling interactive evaluation and training at scale. By shifting focus from task capability to cognitive alignment, my research aims to reposition AI from a tool for task execution to a system for cognitive empowerment.

## Speaker Bio

Yoonjoo Lee is a Postdoctoral Fellow in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Michigan, advised by Prof. Q. Vera Liao. She earned her PhD in Computer Science from KAIST. Her research lies at the intersection of HCI and NLP, where she builds and evaluates AI systems that model and adapt to human cognitive processes, repositioning AI from a tool for task completion to a system that empowers human thinking. She publishes at top HCI and NLP venues, including CHI, UIST, FAccT, ICML, NeurIPS, EMNLP, COLM, and NAACL, receiving paper awards at NAACL '25, EMNLP '25, and CHI '26. She has gained industry experience through internships at Adobe Research, the Allen Institute for AI, and LG AI Research, and serves as an Area Chair and Associate Chair for major HCI and NLP conferences.
