## Abstract

Mobile health technologies are increasingly deployed to improve care delivery, expand access, and strengthen health systems. Yet their value depends not only on technical performance, but on how well they integrate into existing workflows and practices, institutional structures, and sociocultural contexts. In this talk, I present a sociotechnical approach to designing and evaluating mobile health in complex health systems. I discuss how I have applied this approach across projects addressing global health challenges at different stages of technology maturity, from evaluating the security of national HIV data systems in Malawi to iteratively developing a contraceptive decision-support tool in Kenya. These cases illustrate how stakeholder engagement, workflow integration, and attention to technological risks and opportunities shape the design of mobile health. I conclude by reflecting on future directions for applying a sociotechnical approach to scaling mobile health responsibly and integrating technologies into broader care ecosystems.

## Speaker Bio

Lisa Orii is a final-year PhD candidate in the Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science & Engineering at the University of Washington, advised by Dr. Richard Anderson. Her research advances sociotechnical approaches to designing, developing, and evaluating mobile health systems in low- and middle-income countries. Her work has been published at leading Human-Computer Interaction venues, including ACM CHI and ACM CSCW, and received a Best Paper Honorable Mention at CHI. Her research has been recognized through honors such as the Quad Fellowship and MIT Technology Review's Innovators Under 35 Japan. Prior to her PhD, she earned her undergraduate degree in Computer Science and Philosophy from Wellesley College.
