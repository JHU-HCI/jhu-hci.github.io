## Abstract

In recent years, the perception of aging has shifted towards active aging, emphasizing a more dynamic and engaged perspective rather than viewing aging as a disability or the end of life. This transformation has influenced the design of technology and increased the use of co-design to actively engage older adults in shaping and critiquing emerging technologies. I will discuss co-design practices in creating I.R.I.S. (Interactive Robot for Ikigai Support) with older adults in this talk. My talk will show how we can incorporate co-design to promote active aging while gathering valuable design feedback for a socially interactive robot.

## Speaker Bio

Long-Jing Hsu is currently a fourth year Ph.D. student at Indiana University Bloomington (IUB). She earned her Master of Science from National Taiwan University and is expected to complete her Ph.D. in the Spring of 2025. Her research focuses on designing robots to support older adults and their care partners, with an emphasis on care-oriented co-design methods. Since joining Indiana University, she has published 15 peer-reviewed papers and posters at prominent venues (e.g., CHI, HRI, DIS, and IEEE RO-MAN). She received the Luddy Outstanding Research Award in 2024 and won two student design competitions in HRI. She is an advocate for amplifying the voices of older adults in research and technology design.
