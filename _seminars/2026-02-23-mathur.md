## Abstract

As AI systems become increasingly embedded in clinical and everyday health contexts, their ability to generate accurate predictions is no longer sufficient. These systems must also communicate and explain their reasoning in ways that support informed action, calibrated trust and sustained long-term engagement. In this talk, I present a human-centered approach to AI explainability that aims to bridge foundational work in model transparency with longitudinal evaluation of how explanations function in real-world settings. Drawing on deployments of conversational AI systems in assisted living environments and empirical studies of explanation preferences with older adults, I examine how context-aware explanations derived from users? own multimodal data can influence understanding and reliance over time. My work argues for treating explainability as a user-centered design and evaluation challenge that integrates data sources, interface design and behavioral outcomes. I conclude by outlining future directions for adaptive explanation mechanisms in clinical and embedded AI systems.

## Speaker Bio

Niharika Mathur is a Ph.D. candidate in Human-Centered Computing (HCC) at Georgia Tech. Her research lies at the intersection of human-centered AI, explainable AI and health informatics. She designs and evaluates context-aware explanation mechanisms for AI systems embedded in real-world settings, including assisted living communities and clinical environments. Through longitudinal field deployments and mixed-methods studies, her work examines how explanations influence users? understanding, trust and sustained engagement with AI. Her research has been published in leading venues in HCI including CHI, CSCW, ASSETS, UCAml and GROUP, and has received a Best Paper Award at ACM ASSETS. Niharika is also a Foley Scholar recipient and a George Family Foundation Fellow for Innovation in Health Systems at Georgia Tech, where she is advised by Dr. Elizabeth Mynatt and Dr. Sonia Chernova.
