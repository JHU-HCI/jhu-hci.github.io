## Abstract

Emerging AI-enabled technologies?such as intelligent voice assistants (e.g., Alexa) and generative AI chatbots (e.g., ChatGPT)?have reshaped the ways in which individuals interact with technology, access information, or perform small scale everyday tasks. But are these technologies designed for equitable use by everyone? Older adults, as a group, could benefit greatly from such technologies due to the conversational nature of interaction. Yet, often unintentionally, digital ageism manifests in not only perpetuating harmful stereotypes of aging but also in the exclusion of older adults? needs and preferences in the design and development of AI-based technologies. Drawing on examples from AI-based conversational assistants, this talk will explore the various ways these technologies marginalize older adults?from encoding reductive assumptions about aging, to introducing data-driven accessibility barriers, to reinforcing more subtle forms of 'othering' through dominant design practices. I will conclude by discussing both the challenges and the opportunities involved in meaningfully involving older adults in the design of emerging AI systems

## Speaker Bio

Alisha Pradhan is an Assistant Professor in the Ying Wu College of Computing at the New Jersey Institute of Technology. She received her PhD from the University of Maryland, College Park. Her research in Human Computer Interaction (HCI) examines inclusive design of technology for and with older adults and people with disabilities. Her research is published at top-tier HCI conferences, such as, the ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI) and journals, such as, Computer Supported Cooperative Work and Social Computing (CSCW), ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction and has been recognized Best Paper and Honorable Mention awards.
