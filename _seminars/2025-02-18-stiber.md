## Abstract

Effective human-machine interaction requires machines that can interpret and adapt to human behavior in real-time. My research advances human-aware intelligent machines by using social signals as inputs for understanding human internal states and models. My approach is to examine human reactions to misalignment?moments when users? mental models about the machine, task, or world diverge from reality. Misalignments can cause interaction breakdowns but also provides useful information about task context and user perception. I apply this approach in two areas: providing machines with error awareness and with user confusion awareness in physically situated tasks. To achieve this, I build models that use behavioral signals to detect errors and user confusion, develop proactive, multimodal interactive systems to engage users in the error detection process, and demonstrate that behavioral signals?especially when contextualized?can be used for flexible detection of these misalignments. This research is a step towards deploying human-aware machines that can adapt to user behaviors, improving user performance, reliability and acceptance of interactive systems in everyday environments.

## Speaker Bio

Maia Stiber is a PhD graduand in Computer Science at Johns Hopkins University in the Intuitive Computing Lab. Her research focuses on modeling human behaviors to develop human-aware capabilities in HMI, specifically by modeling implicit human responses to unexpected robot errors for improved error detection. She has interned at Microsoft Research and holds a B.S. in Computer Science from Caltech and an M.S.E. in Computer Science from JHU.
